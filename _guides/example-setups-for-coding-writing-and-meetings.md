A blank Workspace Manager can be hard to start from. Here are four setups to copy, then bend to your own apps.

Each recipe names a layout from the palette in Workspace Manager, says which app goes in each region, and what happens to everything else. The apps are examples: swap in the ones you actually use. If you haven't built a setup before, [How to Create Your First Setup](/guides/create-your-first-setup) shows where each button is.

## Code

For writing and running code.

**One display, Main + Stack:**

- Large region: your editor (Xcode, VS Code, or whichever you use).
- Top of the stack: Terminal.
- Bottom of the stack: a browser for documentation and the app you're building.

**With a second display, Two Rows:**

- Top: a second browser window, for the app you're testing. Chrome, Firefox and Safari can fill more than one region.
- Bottom: Slack or your team's chat.

**Everything else:** hide. Add an exception for a music app if you like it visible.

**Shortcut:** <kbd>⇧⌘</kbd> plus a letter. Many people pick the setup's first letter; if an app you use already owns that combination, Flincth tells you and you can pick another.

The clipboard keeps what you copy here — commands, error messages, IDs — out of your other work.

## Writing

For drafting with your research beside you.

**One display, Two Columns:**

- Left: your writing app (Pages, Word, Ulysses, iA Writer…).
- Right: a browser with your sources.

On an ultrawide, try three regions instead: notes on the left, the draft in a wide centre, sources on the right. [Window Layouts for Two Displays or an Ultrawide](/guides/layouts-for-two-displays-and-ultrawide-monitors) shows how.

**Everything else:** hide, including chat. That's half the point.

The clipboard becomes a list of the quotes and links you gathered for this piece, and nothing else.

## Meeting

For calls where you also take notes.

**One display, Two Columns:**

- Left, the wider of the two: your video call app (Zoom, Teams, Google Meet in a browser…).
- Right: Notes, or wherever you write things down.

Drag the line between the regions so the call gets about two thirds of the screen.

**Everything else:** hide, so nothing private is on screen if you share it. Add an exception for your calendar if you want it in view.

Before sharing your screen, <kbd>⌃⌥⌘H</kbd> (**Hide All Apps**) is a quick way to clear everything.

## Research

For reading across several sources at once.

**One display, Four Quarters:**

- Top left and top right: two browser windows.
- Bottom left: Preview, for PDFs.
- Bottom right: Notes.

**Everything else:** hide.

Everything you copy while reading stays in Research's clipboard list, ready when you switch to Writing and open **All**.

## Make them yours

- **Right-click a setup and choose Duplicate Workspace** to start a variation without rebuilding it, for example a laptop-only version of Code.
- **Keep setups few.** Three or four that you actually use beat ten you have to remember.
- **Name them for the work, not the apps.** *Client A* tells you more than *Safari + Notes*.

## What switching does

When you choose a setup, Flincth opens any of its apps that aren't running, moves their windows into place on the desktop you're on, and hides the rest. It never quits anything, and **↩ Not Flincth** in the menu puts your desk back the way it was.
