Flincth Speed Reader shows text in one fixed spot so your eyes stop jumping along the line. The mode decides how much appears at once. Switch at any time with **Word**, **Phrase** and **Sentence** at the bottom of the reader; your place doesn't move.

## Word

One word at a time, with a focus letter marked so your eye lands in the same place on every word. Turn on **Focus guides** in Reader settings for two faint marks above and below that point.

**Good for:** training your speed, light reading, short passages.

## Phrase

A few words that belong together. Flincth splits each sentence at punctuation such as commas, and keeps phrases short, so you take in a whole idea in one glance.

**Good for:** most books and articles. It is usually the easiest mode to follow at higher speeds.

## Sentence

A short sentence, or a long one split into parts. A chunk never runs into the next sentence.

**Good for:** dense or technical text, where you want each thought complete before moving on.

## How Flincth keeps the sense

Whatever the mode:

- It pauses a little after a comma, longer at the end of a sentence and longest at the end of a paragraph.
- A long word gets a little more time than a short one.
- A new chapter or section shows its title on its own first.
- Each time you start or resume, the speed rises from a gentler pace to your target over the first few seconds.

The splitting uses punctuation and simple rules, not AI, so it works in any language written with spaces between words.

## Fine-tuning with Pro

In Settings → Reading & Appearance (or **More reading settings** in the reader), Pro adds:

- **Chunk size:** the longest phrase and the longest sentence part, in words.
- **Pauses (× one word):** how long to wait after a comma, a sentence, a paragraph and at a new section.
- **Ramp-up:** how long the start-up takes and the speed it starts from.
- **Show surrounding text while playing:** the text before and after, faintly, above and below the current words.

The pause view, with the whole paragraph, and sentence back and forward are always free.
