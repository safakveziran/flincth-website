It depends on your version of macOS. Since **macOS Tahoe (26)**, Spotlight keeps a history of what you copy. Before that, the Mac only ever held the last thing you copied.

## macOS Tahoe: clipboard history in Spotlight

1. Press <kbd>⌘Space</kbd> to open Spotlight.
2. Press <kbd>⌘4</kbd> to switch to **Clipboard**.
3. The first time, macOS asks whether to turn it on. If you dismissed that, look for the clipboard option in **System Settings → Spotlight**.

Type to search what you copied. Double-click an item to paste it where your cursor is.

Passwords copied from Apple Passwords are left out.

## macOS Sequoia and earlier: the last item only

Older versions keep one item. To see it, open Finder and choose **Edit → Show Clipboard**. Copy something new and the old item is gone.

## When a clipboard manager helps

A clipboard manager keeps a longer history, opens with a shortcut from any app, and lets you search and paste with the keyboard. It is worth one if you:

- use macOS Sonoma or Sequoia, which have no built-in history,
- copy the same links, commands or replies again and again,
- want history kept for days or weeks rather than a short while.

Things to check before you pick one: whether it skips passwords, where it stores the history (on your Mac or in a cloud), and whether it keeps images or only text.

## A separate history for each kind of work

Flincth Workspace Manager includes a clipboard history with a twist: it keeps a separate list for each setup. What you copy while coding stays with *Code*; quotes and links gathered for an article stay with *Writing*. Switch setups and the right list is waiting.

- Press <kbd>⇧⌘V</kbd> from any app to open it, type to search, press <kbd>Return</kbd> to choose.
- It keeps text (plain, formatted, links and file paths), not images.
- It never records items your password manager marks as confidential.
- The history stays on your Mac.

It works on macOS 14 and later, including versions without a built-in history.
