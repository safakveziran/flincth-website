macOS Sequoia (15) and later can tile windows from the keyboard. This guide lists the keys, how to send a window to another display, and where the built-in tools stop.

## Tile one window

Click the window first, then press:

| Shortcut | What it does |
|---|---|
| <kbd>fn</kbd> <kbd>⌃</kbd> <kbd>←</kbd> / <kbd>→</kbd> | Left or right half of the screen |
| <kbd>fn</kbd> <kbd>⌃</kbd> <kbd>↑</kbd> / <kbd>↓</kbd> | Top or bottom half |
| <kbd>fn</kbd> <kbd>⌃</kbd> <kbd>F</kbd> | Fill the screen (not full screen) |
| <kbd>fn</kbd> <kbd>⌃</kbd> <kbd>C</kbd> | Centre the window |
| <kbd>fn</kbd> <kbd>⌃</kbd> <kbd>R</kbd> | Return to the size it had before tiling |

Add <kbd>⇧</kbd> to an arrow shortcut to arrange two windows at once: the active window on one side and the previous one on the other.

Quarters have no shortcut. Use **Window → Move & Resize** in the menu bar; holding <kbd>⌥</kbd> while the menu is open shows more arrangements. On newer Mac keyboards the <kbd>fn</kbd> key is the Globe key.

## Tile with the mouse

Drag a window to the edge of the screen until an outline appears, then let go. Drag to a corner for a quarter. Holding <kbd>⌥</kbd> while dragging shows the outline straight away.

You can turn these off, or the margins between tiled windows, in **System Settings → Desktop & Dock**.

## Move a window to another display

With more than one display connected, the **Window** menu (under **Move & Resize** in Sequoia and later) offers **Move to** followed by the other display's name. You can also drag the window across.

## Before Sequoia

macOS Sonoma and earlier have no tiling keys. Hovering over the green button in a window's corner offers **Tile Window to Left/Right of Screen**, which puts two apps side by side in full screen on a new desktop.

## Where the built-in keys stop

The shortcuts arrange the window you are in, one or two at a time. They don't:

- arrange every app across several displays in one step,
- remember an arrangement or put it back after a monitor reconnects,
- open the apps you need or hide the ones you don't.

That is what Flincth Workspace Manager does. A **setup** places each app's windows across all your screens and switches its clipboard history; give it a shortcut such as <kbd>⇧⌘D</kbd> and one press arranges the whole desk. It works alongside the tiling keys rather than replacing them.
