It's the middle of November, and the reading goal you set in January is looking optimistic. The pile on your nightstand hasn't moved since summer. There are about seven weeks left.

The good news: seven weeks is more reading time than it feels like. This post works out what a book actually takes, where to find the minutes, which books to pick, and how to read some of them faster, honestly, without pretending you can read a novel in an hour.

---

## 1. Do the math on one book

Most people overestimate how long a book takes.

A typical novel runs somewhere around 70,000 to 100,000 words. Adults read fiction at about **260 words per minute** on average, according to [a large 2019 review of reading speed](/blog/what-speed-to-use-in-a-speed-reading-app). So an 80,000-word novel takes roughly:

- **5 hours** at 260 wpm,
- **4 hours** at 330 wpm.

Five hours. That's not a holiday's worth of reading; it's a week of evenings.

---

## 2. Find the minutes, not the hours

You don't need long, quiet afternoons. You need small pockets you already have:

- the commute, or the wait before a meeting starts,
- fifteen minutes before bed instead of one more scroll,
- the first coffee of the day.

Twenty minutes a day for seven weeks is about **16 hours** of reading. At an ordinary pace, that's three average novels, or four or five shorter books. The trick is to make the book easier to reach than your phone's other apps: keep it on the device that's already in your hand.

---

## 3. Pick books that fit the time you have

A reading list isn't a contract. To finish more, choose better:

- **Finish what you started.** A half-read book is the cheapest one to complete.
- **Go shorter.** Novellas, essay collections and short non-fiction count just as much on a list, and finishing one builds momentum.
- **Drop what you're not enjoying.** Fifty pages is a fair trial. Life is long; the year isn't.
- **Mix weights.** Pair one demanding book with one easy one, and read the easy one on tired evenings.

---

## 4. Read the lighter books faster

Speed reading has a reputation it partly deserves. Research finds that [comprehension falls as speed rises](/blog/do-speed-reading-apps-work), especially with dense material. Nobody should speed read a contract, a textbook chapter they'll be tested on, or a poem.

But a lot of what's on a reading list isn't like that. A thriller, a memoir, a popular-science book you mostly want the gist of: these can go a bit faster than your usual pace without losing the story.

That's what an RSVP reader is for. [Flincth Speed Reader](/ios/speed-reader) shows your own book one word, phrase or short sentence at a time, in one fixed spot, so your eyes stop jumping along the line. A few things help:

- **Start near your normal speed.** If you don't know it, 250 wpm is a good first try. Raise it about 25 wpm at a time; [here's how to tell when you've gone too far](/blog/what-speed-to-use-in-a-speed-reading-app).
- **Try phrase mode** for fiction. Short groups of words keep the rhythm of a sentence better than single words. [This guide compares the three modes](/guides/ios/word-phrase-or-sentence-mode).
- **Pause whenever you need to.** Tap, and you see the text around where you are. Your place is saved for next time.
- **Switch back to normal reading for the hard parts.** A faster pace for the easy chapters leaves more time for the slow ones.

## 5. Read what you already own

Plenty of good books are already on your devices or free to download.

Speed Reader opens DRM-free **EPUB**, **PDF** with a text layer, **DOCX**, **TXT**, **RTF** and **Markdown** files, and text you paste in. [This guide shows how to add one](/guides/ios/speed-read-epub-and-pdf-on-iphone).

Books bought from Kindle or Apple Books are protected with DRM, and no third-party app can open them; [here's what works and what doesn't](/blog/can-you-speed-read-kindle-books). For classics, Project Gutenberg and Standard Ebooks publish thousands of public-domain books as free, DRM-free EPUBs.

## The short version

1. One average novel takes about five hours at a normal pace.
2. Twenty minutes a day for seven weeks is about sixteen hours.
3. Finish what you started, go shorter, and drop what you don't enjoy.
4. Read the lighter books a little faster, and slow down for the dense ones.
5. Start with the DRM-free books you already have.

Seven weeks is enough. Pick the next book tonight.
